# Eye Gaze mode: the user's eyeball direction aims Quaddle's pupils

## Context

CamWalkingGame drives a Petoi Quaddle robot from a webcam. Earlier today we built
**Body Follow** (branch `feature/eye-contact`, 24 commits, pushed but **not merged**):
torso position aims the robot's on-screen pupils, the body leans, and it takes bounded
walk steps on approach or retreat. That works and is the demo fallback.

This adds a fourth mode whose signal is **where your eyes point**, not where your body is.
The pose model loaded today is blind to this: BlazePose gives eye corners and an eye
centre but no iris, so it cannot see gaze. This needs MediaPipe FaceLandmarker.

**Confirmed decisions:** fourth mode on the dial; built for tomorrow; Body Follow left
untouched as fallback; mirror semantics matching Body Follow; share the wire-level send
layer with separate tuning; and **the tracked quantity is eye rotation relative to the
head**, not gaze relative to the camera.

### What that last decision means, precisely

Hold a fixed point on the wall and turn your head: your eyeballs counter-rotate to stay
on it, and this mode **will report that rotation and move the robot's pupils**. That is
correct behaviour here, not a defect. We are not building gaze-relative-to-camera, which
would need a second head-pose estimator layered on top. The demo-legible effect is: hold
your head still, flick your eyes, the robot follows.

Document this in the help text. Do not claim head-pose independence.

## Scope constraint that removes three whole failure classes

**One robot endpoint at a time for this mode.** Either serial or a single WebSocket robot.

Three of the review's critical findings (per-destination view activation, `linkWasUp`
being a single global that cannot represent serial-plus-WiFi, and `releaseEyeLook`
marking everything released when *any* destination succeeds) all stem from one global
link state serving multiple endpoints. Per-endpoint view/release/generation tracking is
the correct general fix and is not a one-day change. Constraining the mode is.

Enforce it: if more than one endpoint is connected when entering gaze mode, show a hint
and refuse to stream. Multi-endpoint gaze is the first follow-up.

## The estimator

Keep it in a **pure function** `gazeFromLandmarks(lm, aspect) → {hx, rsy, perEye, reason}`.
Nothing else is testable without a camera, and the review was right that this is the only
way to validate the math offline.

### Aspect correction comes first

Normalized landmark x and y are **not isotropic** on a non-square video, so Euclidean
lengths and projections computed from them directly are distorted. Multiply x by
`videoWidth / videoHeight` (or work in pixels) **before** any distance or dot product.
Without this the roll-invariance claim is false.

### Per-eye ratio, with both axes oriented the same way

```
// aspect-corrected 2-vectors
axis  = outerCanthus - innerCanthus
if (axis.x < 0) axis = -axis          // orient BOTH eyes toward increasing image x
mid   = (outerCanthus + innerCanthus) / 2
w     = |axis|
ratio = dot(iris - mid, axis / w) / w
```

**The explicit re-orientation is load-bearing.** `outer − inner` points in *opposite*
image directions for the two eyes (subject's right eye has its outer canthus at smaller
x), so without the flip, averaging the two ratios partially cancels real gaze. The
previous draft said "oriented toward increasing image x" in a comment without
implementing it. Test: equal image-space iris displacement in both eyes must produce
same-signed ratios.

Guard before dividing: reject non-finite coordinates and `w` below a floor.

### Mirror semantics are free

Body Follow computes its aim from **raw normalized x** (the preview's `scaleX(-1)` is
CSS-only). Looking to your own right moves both irises to smaller image x → negative
ratio; stepping to your own right also gives negative. **Same polarity**, so `signX: 1`
and the two modes cannot disagree. Recorded so nobody "fixes" it later.

### Both eyes, and what happens when only one is usable

Average both when both are valid: each iris rests slightly temporal of its socket
midpoint, and that per-person bias is **antisymmetric between the eyes**, so it cancels
in the mean.

**One eye is sufficient** when the other is rejected — that is a deliberate choice, not a
contradiction. Bias cancellation is lost, so the output carries a small per-person offset;
expose `EYE_GAZE_TUNE.biasX` (default 0) rather than inventing a correction we cannot
validate today. Status line must show which eyes are in use so the operator can see it.

**Do not gate on `visibility`.** The mesh does not predict visibility; it emits 0 for
every point and *hallucinates* occluded irises rather than omitting them. Use geometric
proxies: reject an eye whose socket width is under ~55% of the other's; treat
eye-aspect-ratio below ~0.15 as a blink; treat cross-eye disagreement above ~0.12 as
one-eye-bad.

**Every hold is capped.** A blink or disagreement holds the last *valid* value for at most
`EYE_GAZE_HOLD_MS` (start 300ms). Past that it is a loss, not a hold. Critically, the
grace window expires against **the last valid gaze observation**, not against frame
arrival — otherwise a face that keeps being detected with unusable irises refreshes the
watchdog forever while the robot holds a stale stick.

### Scale, smoothing, and honest error bars

- **Full scale 0.15.** Iris travel is about `12mm · sin θ` against a ~29mm fissure, so
  ±20° of eye rotation (where people start recruiting head turn) gives ratio ≈ ±0.14.
  So `gain` defaults to **1.0**, not Body Follow's 3.6.
- **Elapsed-time smoothing, not a fixed α.** A fixed coefficient changes its time
  constant with inference rate. Use `α = 1 - exp(-dt / τ)` with `τ ≈ 250ms`.
- **The noise numbers are estimates, not measurements.** Predicted raw jitter is ~9 stick
  units; the filter should bring that to roughly **2.3 units RMS**, not a ≤2 range as the
  previous draft claimed. Measure it in Stage 1 and set τ from the measurement. Filtering
  cannot remove systematic bias or correlated fitting error.
- **Reuse `EYE_DEADBAND = 2` and `EYE_MIN_MAG = 3` unchanged.** The centred floor matters
  more here than in Body Follow: "looking straight at the robot" is the commonest state in
  a gaze demo and must not hand the eyes back to idle saccades.
- **Vertical: ship `gainY = 0`.** Vertical iris travel is about half the horizontal; the
  upper lid *follows* vertical gaze so the socket reference moves with the signal it is
  meant to normalise; and the firmware divides vertical response by 10 up and 2 down, so
  it renders as a crouch rather than a tilt. Exposed so it can be explored on hardware.

## Shared send layer: what must change in it

The review found two defects that **already exist in Body Follow** and would be inherited
rather than introduced. Both are worth fixing in the shared layer, which fixes Body Follow
at the same time.

1. **Late completions can clobber newer state.** `releaseEyeLook()` clears `inFlight`
   while older sends are still pending; when those settle, their handlers can clear a
   newer command's lock or set `sendFailed` against it, and a late release completion can
   overwrite a newer mode's `lastSentHx` / `releasedQ` and suppress later cleanup. Fix:
   a monotonic `eyeState.gen`, incremented on mode change and on teardown; every
   completion handler captures `gen` at dispatch and mutates nothing if it no longer
   matches.
2. **`ve` dispatch is treated as activation.** If every `ve` send rejects, only the stick
   cache is invalidated and the next frame can stream `J` with no view guard. Fix: track
   `viewOkQ` separately, set only when a `ve` send *resolves*, and refuse to send any stick
   while it is false. With the single-endpoint constraint above, this is one flag rather
   than a per-destination map.

Everything else on `eyeState` is genuinely link-level and shared correctly:
`lastSentHx/Rsy`, `lastSendMs`, `inFlight`, `releasedQ`, `quiet`, `lastFrameMs`. Two
watchdogs racing to send zeros down one transport with one in-flight slot would be worse
than any corruption it prevents.

`releaseEyeLook()` must also clear `gazeState`, not only the body filter.

## "Reuse" means reuse the primitives, not paste the block

The review was right that the send block has pose-specific coupling. Specifically:

- Its status lines reference `target`, `cands` and pose-derived values that do not exist in
  gaze mode → **write a gaze-specific status block** (`mode=gaze`, per-eye ratios, which
  eyes are in use, the `stick:` readout). That readout is the entire tuning interface.
- It updates `eyeState.lastFrameMs` in a tail that sits next to the walk call → gaze
  **must** update `lastFrameMs` itself, or the watchdog releases the stick every 500ms and
  the mode silently never works.
- The no-target branch needs gaze's own valid-observation timestamp and reacquisition
  reset, per the hold rules above.

Genuinely reusable as-is: `sendEyeCommand`, `sendEyeStick`, `releaseEyeLook`, the
reconnect-edge invalidation, the `sendFailed` cache invalidation, the `moved`/send-interval
gate with its dispatch-result check, and the watchdog itself.

## Mode wiring

Same four-way pattern already applied when Body Follow made these three-way. Every
`trackMode` test needs `"gaze"`: the dial button and its click handler,
`trackModeTitleKey`, `applyTrackModeChrome`, `setTrackMode` validation and its leave-mode
release, `tick()`'s model guard and dispatch, `startCamera`/`stopCamera`,
`closeSerialPort`, and `refreshCameraButton`'s ternary becoming a lookup.

> **The one line that silently breaks everything:** the watchdog tests
> `trackMode !== "eye"`. Not widening it to include `"gaze"` makes the 500ms watchdog
> treat every gaze frame as a foreign mode and release the stick continuously. The mode
> appears to do nothing, with no error.

**Entering gaze mode must stop any active walk** (`eyeStopWalk`), since stepping is not
available here and a gait must not outlive the mode that started it.

**Isolate the face model's failure.** Wrap `initFace` so a missing or failing face model
cannot reject or stall `initPose` and disable Body Follow and the other two modes. Model
readiness must be per-model: gaze's dial button disabled when `faceLandmarker` is null,
while the others stay available. **Test with the model file deliberately absent** — an
unchanged `renderEyeTracking` diff does not prove the fallback survives when init, shared
state and teardown all changed.

Stepping stays unavailable: the depth signal is shoulder width from the *pose* model and
gaze mode runs the *face* model, where shoulders do not exist. `refreshEyeWalkButton`
already disables it for any mode other than `"eye"`, so this costs zero lines — assert it
in review.

### Findings

**Architecture**

- `[P1] (confidence: 9/10) app/index.html:7317-7321, 7330 — setTrackMode destroys the release
  retry guarantee synchronously.` `setTrackMode` calls `releaseEyeLook()` **without awaiting**,
  then line 7330 calls `resetEyeTrackingState()`, which sets `eyeState.releasedQ = true`
  (`:6210`) while those zero writes are still pending. The comment at `:6268-6270` claims "if
  every transport rejects, releasedQ stays false and the no-target branch (and the stall
  watchdog) keep retrying until one lands" — but the watchdog only retries `if
  (!eyeState.releasedQ)` (`:7482`), and that flag has already been forced true. Switching out
  of Body Follow on a flaky link leaves the pupils frozen off-centre until a power cycle.
  This is item 1 of `docs/body-follow-todos.md` ("If this is broken the pupils freeze
  off-centre until a power cycle, which is a visible demo failure") and it is broken.
  **The plan's `gen` counter does not fix this** — `gen` guards late async completions; this
  clobber is synchronous and unconditional. → **R1**

- `[P1] (confidence: 9/10) app/index.html:6713 — the view guard is armed on dispatch, not on
  delivery.` `if (sendEyeCommand(encodeCommand("v", ["e"]))) eyeState.viewAssertedMs = now;`
  — `sendEyeCommand` returns true as soon as it has something to push (`:6248-6257`), so a `ve`
  whose writes all reject buys 5 seconds (`EYE_VIEW_REASSERT_MS`, `:6096`) of unguarded `J`
  streaming, which `processJoystick` routes into the legs. The plan already names this and the
  `viewOkQ` fix is right. **One correction to the plan's wording:** a resolved serial write
  proves bytes left the host, not that the firmware set `viewMode == 3`. There is no read-back,
  so `viewOkQ` is "the write resolved", not a view guarantee. Do not document it as one.

- `[P1] (confidence: 9/10) docs/body-follow-todos.md:34-42 vs this plan's "~2.3 units RMS" —
  the plan never connects its own error budget to the leg-jitter constraint.` Gaze and lean are
  one firmware signal: `doSyncBody` feeds rendered gaze back through
  `reactionApplyRstickFromEyeNorm()`, so **eye flicks drive leg joints 4 and 5**. Petoi's
  recorded guidance is that constraining those joints "是为了防止抖动时晃动太剧烈翻车" — stop a
  noisy signal being amplified into body sway and tipping; the real requirement is "don't feed
  joints 4/5 a noisy signal." This plan predicts ~2.3 units RMS after filtering, roughly double
  Body Follow's filtered ~1.1, and routes it to the same joints. The plan says "Stepping stays
  unavailable" but never says the body still leans, nor that the noisier signal makes that lean
  worse. → **R2**

- `[P2] (confidence: 8/10) app/index.html:7171-7175 — a face-model load inserted into initPose's
  serial chain delays the Start button for every mode.` `await initHand(vision)` sits at `:7171`
  and `refreshCameraButton()` at `:7175`, so an `await initFace(vision)` placed alongside blocks
  the Start button on the extra ~3.8MB model load even when the operator only wants Body Follow.
  That degrades the demo fallback. Fix: call `refreshCameraButton()` before `initFace`, or fire
  `initFace` without awaiting it and refresh on completion. `initHand` (`:7120-7145`) is the
  pattern to copy exactly: it swallows its own errors, so it can never reject into `initPose`'s
  catch, which sets `modelFailed` and `showError` and would read as total app failure. This is
  necessary implementation of the plan's existing "Isolate the face model's failure" commitment,
  recorded as a correction rather than a new choice.

- `[P3] (confidence: 7/10) — three resident models.` pose 5.8MB + hand 7.8MB + face ~3.8MB. Only
  one runs per frame (mode-exclusive dispatch, `:6945-6954`), so there is no per-frame cost, but

- **DR3 — A persistent, self-dismissing prompt teaches the control** (D12, "Persistent,
  self-dismissing"). On entering Eye Gaze with a face detected, show a centred two-line prompt via
  the existing persistent-prompt path: `gazeReadyFlash` = EN "Hold your head still" / "Now move
  just your eyes", ZH "头部保持不动" / "只用眼睛看左右". It stays up until the smoothed gaze ratio
  exceeds a threshold (start 0.03, the same value that releases R4's centred hold), then clears and
  does not return for the session unless the face is lost and reacquired. Reuses `persistReadyFlash`
  and the `ready-long` two-line lane added earlier today.

  **Why this is the highest-value item in the design review:** moving your body does almost nothing
  in this mode, and the natural next move, turning your head, produces the behavior approved under
  the eyes-relative-to-head semantics, where the eyes counter-rotate and the pupils follow. That
  looks exactly like head tracking. Without this prompt the most likely outcome is that a visitor
  concludes Eye Gaze is the same mode as Body Follow and never finds the feature.
